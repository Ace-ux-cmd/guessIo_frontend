guessIo Game Guide

A complete, plain-language guide to how guessIo works: match rules, difficulty modes, progression, reserved players, accounts, and every screen in the app.

Section 01

What Is guessIo?

guessIo is a real-time, competitive, multiplayer code-breaking game. It runs as a web app / installable Progressive Web App (PWA), working in a browser on phone or desktop or a downloadable mobile app.

The one-line pitch, taken from the app's own install listing:

"Analyse → Outsmart → Crack the Code."

Genre

A logic-deduction game where everyone races head-to-head to crack the same code.

Look & feel

A colored tile grid where every tile is a digit (0-9), never a letter.

Tone

Sharp, dark and competitive. A precision duel of logic.

Section 02

How a Match Works: The Core Loop

The secret code

  • Each round has one secret code, generated by the system: a string of unique digits (0-9, no repeats), a length that depends on the mode.
  • A leading zero is allowed: a code like 0473 is valid.
  • One shared code per round. Every player at the table is trying to crack the same code; nobody is guessing an opponent's personal code. True in every mode, including 2-player, 3-player, 4-player, and solo Practice Mode.

Starting a match and between rounds

  • When a match is found, a 5-second countdown runs before round 1.
  • After each round (unless the match just ended), a Round Result screen shows for 5 seconds, then the next round starts automatically. It shows who cracked the code (or "Round drawn"), how many turns it took, the revealed code, the running score, the round time and the fastest guess.

Turn order

  • Players take turns in a fixed rotation for that round.
  • The rotation's starting player changes each round (round 2 starts with whoever was second in round 1, and so on), so no one player is stuck always going last.

Making a guess

On your turn, you enter a guess of the same length as the secret code, using unique digits (no repeats allowed in your guess either). After you submit, you get feedback on every digit you guessed:

Feedback Meaning Standard-mode color
Correct That digit is in the code, in the exact position you guessed Green
Present That digit is in the code, but in a different position Yellow
Wrong That digit is not in the code at all Gray

  • This feedback is private: in online matches, only you see the color-coded result of your own guesses. Other players see what digits you guessed, not what the colors meant for you. Exception: In local Pass & Play mode, both players share a single screen, making all feedback visible to everyone. However, the core gameplay ensures that feedback only displays for the active player whose turn it currently is.
  • The guess limit is a shared budget for the whole round, not a per-player allowance. If it runs out before anyone cracks the code, the round is a draw; nobody scores it.
  • The first player to guess the exact code wins that round.

Turn timer

  • Each turn has a time limit; 30 seconds by default. If it expires, that player's turn is skipped (no guess submitted) and turn passes to the next person.
  • The countdown ring turns amber at 10 seconds left and red at 5 on your own turn.
  • A disconnected player keeps their seat for up to 60 seconds, and their turns run and time out normally. If they don't reconnect within 60 seconds, they forfeit the match and are skipped from then on.

Winning the match: best of 3 rounds

A match is decided over up to 3 rounds:

  • Round 1 alone never decides anything.
  • After round 2: if one player is strictly ahead on rounds won, they win the match immediately.
  • If there's a tie after round 2, for any reason, the match always proceeds to a 3rd, deciding round. Whoever's ahead after round 3 wins; if still tied, the match ends in a draw.
The same rule applies to 3 and 4-player matches: a single player strictly ahead after round 2 wins, otherwise round 3 decides, and if players are still tied after round 3 the match is a draw. A dedicated tie-breaker mechanic for 3+ players (e.g., sudden-death) is planned for a later version, likely alongside tournament support.

Going idle

If a full rotation passes with nobody submitting a valid guess, everyone gets an on-screen warning. If that happens a second time in a row, the entire match (not just the round) is cancelled, and every player still in it is penalized: -30 rating (flat) and -10% of their current Points balance. This rule applies to Casual and Ranked matches alike. Any valid guess resets the idle count.

Disconnecting & reconnecting

  • If you lose connection, you get a 60-second grace window to reconnect before you're automatically counted as having forfeited.
  • Reconnecting restores the match exactly where it is: mid-turn (with the time left on the clock), between rounds, or at the final result if the match already ended. A page refresh or a brief network blip does not knock you out.
  • The app retries automatically 3 times. If that fails, a "Could not reconnect" dialog offers Reconnect (try again) or Play as guest (leave the online session and start an offline guest match).
  • Refreshing or closing the tab during a live match triggers your browser's own "are you sure" prompt.

Quitting on purpose (Forfeit / Resign)

  • A flag button is available throughout a live match. Using it asks for confirmation ("Leaving now counts as a forfeit... this may affect your rating") before it's applied. The Escape key and the device or browser Back button open the same prompt; Back or Escape alone never forfeits.
  • Logging out or signing in on another platform during a match counts as a forfeit: the app warns you about this before logging you out.
  • In a 2-player match, a forfeit ends the match immediately as a win for the other player. In a 3-4 player match, the match simply continues without the player who left (and if only one player remains, that player wins).
Section 03

Difficulty: Standard vs. Hardcore

Both modes are told the exact same underlying information about each guess. The difference is purely in how that information is displayed:

Standard Hardcore
Right digit, right position Shown as its own color (green) Shown in amber, same as any digit in the code
Right digit, wrong position Shown in amber Shown in amber (unchanged)
Digit not in the code Gray Gray (unchanged)

In short: Standard tells you position; Hardcore only tells you presence. You still see how many of your digits belong somewhere in the code; you just work out which slot through further deduction. Because it's harder, a Hardcore win is worth more Points than a Standard win.

Section 04

Player Counts & Round Sizes

Online matches support 2, 3, or 4 players. Code length and the shared guess budget scale with player count:

Players Code length Guess budget per round
2 4 digits 10 guesses
3 5 digits 15 guesses
4 6 digits 20 guesses

Practice/Guest Mode uses the same code lengths but a different guess budget: Easy is 4 digits with 10 guesses, Medium is 5 digits with 12 guesses, and Hard is 6 digits with 15 guesses. Practice can be played against the computer or in local Pass & Play.

Section 05

Ways to Play (Match Types)

There are three ways to start an online match, chosen from the "New match" screen:

Type Affects Rating? Earns Points? Saved to history/stats? How you get matched
Casual No Yes Yes Matchmaking queue
Ranked Yes Yes Yes Matchmaking queue
Private Room No No No Via room code

Casual & Ranked (matchmaking)

  • You pick match type, difficulty, and player count, then join a queue for that exact combination.
  • Matching is first-come-first-served, not based on skill or rating, even in Ranked. Whoever's next in the same queue bucket gets matched together.
  • To ensure matches start quickly, any empty seats are filled gradually with our reserved participants (one every 5 seconds) while the system continues searching for other players. This means a Ranked match can feature reserved stand-ins alongside other players.

Private Rooms

  • The host picks a player count and difficulty and gets a 6-character room code (letters and numbers, excluding the easily-confused I, O, 0, 1).
  • Friends join by entering that code. Player count is locked once the room is created; only the host can start the match, and only once every seat is filled.
  • Difficulty is also locked once the room is created, a read-only field for the life of the room, not something the host can change mid-lobby.
  • Private matches are not saved as match records: no match-history entry, no player-statistics update, no points, and no rating change for anyone, win or lose. The in-match Results screen still displays a rounds-won tally, but there's no "View Match Details" link afterward.
  • If the host leaves before starting, host status passes to the next player in the room automatically.
Section 06

Practice / Guest Mode (No Account Needed)

Anyone can play without creating an account, via a "Practice Mode" setup screen. Two local modes:

Vs Computer

You against the computer, entirely on your device.

Pass & Play

Two people share one device, trading turns.

This runs the same rules engine as online play (same shared code per round, same best-of-3 structure, same Standard/Hardcore feedback rules) but simplified: no turn timers, no disconnect handling, and no idle-cycle cancellation, as there's no active online session to manage.

Instead of a player count, you choose a Round Length, which has its own limits, separate from the online player-count table:

Round Length Code length Guesses
Easy 4 digits 10
Medium 5 digits 12
Hard 6 digits 15

Key differences from online play

  • Nothing is saved. The app repeats this warning at setup ("Playing as guest: progress won't be saved") and again at results ("Guest match: nothing was saved"), and nudges you to create a free account afterward.
  • If you close the tab or app mid-match, your progress is kept on that device only, and next time you'll be explicitly asked whether to resume it or start fresh; never silently resumed or silently thrown away.
Section 07

Reserved Participants ("Temporary Stand-ins")

Reserved players are not random guessers. Their behavior:

  1. Their first guess (or any guess before they've received feedback) is a random valid code.
  2. After that, they mentally narrow down every possible code still consistent with all feedback received so far, and pick randomly from whatever's left. They genuinely deduce; they just don't always make the single mathematically "best" next guess, only a guess that's still logically possible.
  3. They pause 2 to 15 seconds before each guess, so they don't feel instant or robotic giving other players time to think.

Section 08

Progression: Points & Rating

There are two separate progression numbers, tracked and leaderboarded independently.

Points

Earned at the end of every completed match (Except Private):

Result Standard Hardcore
Win +3 +5
Draw +1 +1
Loss +0 +0

In 3 and 4-player matches only the match winner gets the win points. Everyone else, including players who won a round and players who forfeited, counts as a loss.

If a match is cancelled for going idle, everyone remaining loses 10% of their current Points balance (rounded to the nearest whole number).

Rating

A competitive, Elo-style number that only changes as a result of Ranked matches (win/lose/draw affects it; Casual and Private results never move it). Each pair of players in the match is compared head-to-head based on final placement, using a standard Elo expected-score calculation, and the results across all pairings are averaged into one rating change per player.

  • New player accounts start at a Rating of 0. Players start at the same point, so both begin on equal footing.
  • The idle-cancellation penalty (a flat -30 Rating) is applied even in Casual matches, not only Ranked.
  • Placement is based on rounds won in the match: more than 1 round won counts as 1st, exactly 1 as 2nd, and 0 as 3rd. Players in the same tier are treated as tied. This means a match winner can land in the "2nd" tier, for example when round 1 was drawn and they won round 2.
  • Larger lobbies pay more: the averaged result is multiplied by x1.0 for 2 players, x1.25 for 3 players and x1.5 for 4 players.
  • A player who disconnects or forfeits is left out of the comparison and takes a flat -30 Rating instead.
  • In a 2-player Ranked match where the opponent leaves, the remaining player's Rating change is 0 (they still get the win and the Points). This is deliberate, to stop players losing on purpose to feed rating to a friend.

Statistics (tracked per account)

Currently tracked and shown wherever match history/profile stats appear: matches played, matches won, matches lost, matches drawn, rounds won, turns skipped, times disconnected, and times a match was cancelled for inactivity. "Total guesses" and "Match time" on a Results or Match Details screen are always calculated fresh from that specific match, not pulled from a running lifetime total.

Section 10

Leaderboards

Two separate global leaderboards, matching the two progression numbers:

Rating Leaderboard
Points Leaderboard
  • Each shows the top 20 only: a top-3 podium (displayed in 2nd-1st-3rd order left to right) followed by ranks 4 to 20.
  • Players appear by their call sign (display name), not their username.
  • Ties are broken by account age: the older account ranks higher, so ranks are always unique.
  • Your own position is always shown in a "Your position" card, even if you're outside the top 20.
  • Only active accounts are ranked. The Leaderboards screen requires sign-in.

Top 10 status

A Top 10 indicator is a live status tied to current Rating leaderboard rank, not a one-time unlock:

  • Shown only while the player's current Rating rank is within the top 10 and they have played at least 10 Ranked matches.
  • If they drop out of the top 10, the indicator is removed.
  • If they re-enter the top 10 later, it's shown again.

Unlike permanent achievements, this reflects standing at the time it's checked: earned and lost as rank changes, not kept forever. It appears in the Competitive status card on your Profile.

Section 11

Match History & Match Details

Match History (account required) lists your past matches, filterable by All / Ranked / Casual / Wins / Losses, grouped by date ("Today," "Yesterday," then by date), 20 per page with a Load more button. Each row shows the outcome (Win, Loss, Forfeit, Draw or Cancelled), your opponents, match type, difficulty, how long ago, rating change and score. Private matches are never saved, so they don't appear.

Opening an individual match shows:

  • The outcome, match type, difficulty, player count, date and how long the match lasted.
  • A round-by-round breakdown, including every round's secret code: codes are only ever revealed once the match is completely over, never mid-match.
  • Each participant's rounds won, plus your rating change and points earned.
  • Total guesses and match time, calculated specifically for that match.

Matches cancelled for idleness appear as Cancelled, with the penalty rating change.

Section 12

Accounts, Sign-In & Access

  • Signing in is done with Google only. There is no password. You are required to accept the Terms and Privacy Policy before the Google button works (guests are exempt).
  • Choosing a username: a brand-new account picks a username once, before the account is created. It is mandatory, 3 to 30 characters (lowercase letters, numbers and underscores), unique ignoring case, and checked for availability as you type. It can never be changed, and it's what friends search for. The name "guest" is reserved.
  • Call sign: the bold display name shown on matches, lobbies and leaderboards. It starts as your Google name and can be edited (up to 30 characters) from Edit profile. Your @username sits underneath it. Your email is shown read-only.
  • Only one active session per account at a time. Logging in on a new device immediately signs out any other device that was logged in, with an on-screen message explaining why. If you had a match in progress on the session that got signed out, it counts as a forfeit.
  • Settings (opened from the Profile tab) holds Edit profile, Help and feedback, Documentation, Terms of service, Privacy policy, Log out and Delete account. Theme, sound and other preference controls are not part of v1.
  • Deleting your account requires typing your email to confirm. It immediately scrubs your username, email, and display name, and signs you out. The account can never be signed into again. Because match history is shared with opponents, the underlying match/round records are kept (so opponents' history stays intact), just detached from any identifying information about you. Your own Rating, Points, and stats disappear.
  • Guests (no account) can freely use Practice Mode, Help and feedback, and "How to Play." Multiplayer, Leaderboards, Match History and Private Rooms appear as locked "Needs an account" tiles, and tapping one goes straight to sign-in.
  • Suspensions and bans: a suspended or banned account sees an Account Locked screen, even mid-match (it takes effect within about 15 seconds). A suspended player can send one appeal at a time and is emailed the outcome. A ban is permanent and cannot be appealed.

  • Share your feedback: a short form for feedback, bugs and support requests is periodically presented to gather data and improve gaming experience for players
  • Email: write to the developer, or use the "Contact me" link on the Help and feedback screen.
